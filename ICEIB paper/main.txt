\begin{abstract}
Outdoor physical activity confers substantial cardiopulmonary benefits, yet exposure to fine particulate matter ($\text{PM}_{2.5}$) above $25~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ sharply attenuates exercise-induced mortality risk reductions. Existing official air quality services in Taiwan report station-level observations and multi-day regional forecasts, leaving individuals without actionable, hour-by-hour guidance for specific activity windows, while unconstrained large language models (LLMs) suffer from numerical hallucination, sycophantic validation of false user premises, and unsafe medical overreach. To bridge this gap, we propose \textit{AirGuard AI}, an evidence-grounded Domain AI Agent that decouples numerical forecasting, statutory domain rules, and natural-language orchestration across an eight-module architecture. The system ingests Taiwan Ministry of Environment (MOENV) open datasets (\texttt{AQX\_P\_432}, \texttt{AQX\_P\_488}, and \texttt{AQX\_P\_13}), enforces strict temporal causality and unit validation, generates 1-, 3-, and 6-hour station-level $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ forecasts via lightweight time-series regression models benchmarked against Persistence and Seasonal Naive baselines, and constrains recommendations through MOENV's 2025-revised Air Quality Index (AQI) activity guidelines using allowlisted tool calls and deterministic output guardrails. We further specify a rigorous dual-track evaluation protocol combining leakage-safe rolling-origin backtesting on 2024--2026 Taichung monitoring stations with an adversarial 30-scenario safety testbench targeting provenance completeness, rule consistency, and graceful degradation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Domain AI Agent, air quality forecasting, $\text{PM}_{2.5}$, explainable decision support, open environmental data, safety guardrails, Taiwan MOENV.

\section{Introduction}
% ==============================================================================

Ambient air pollution constitutes a critical global public health burden. According to the Health Effects Institute (HEI) \textit{State of Global Air 2024} report, air pollution ranks as the second leading risk factor for global mortality, accounting for approximately 8.1 million deaths worldwide in 2021, with over 90\% (7.8 million deaths) attributable to particulate matter ($\text{PM}_{2.5}$) exposure \cite{b1}. The World Health Organization (WHO) emphasizes that no safe lower threshold exists for ambient exposure and advises maintaining regular physical activity while proactively adjusting the timing and location of outdoor exercise to avoid pollution peaks \cite{b2}, \cite{b3}.

In Taiwan, public participation in outdoor sports has reached record levels: the 2025 National Sports Participation Survey reported that 83.3\% of citizens engage in physical activity and 35.6\% exercise regularly, with jogging and brisk walking consistently ranking among the top three activities \cite{b4}. In central Taiwan's Taichung metropolitan area, long-term environmental governance reduced annual manual-station $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ concentrations from $18.8~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ in 2018 to $12.8$--$12.9~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ in 2025--2026 \cite{b5}, \cite{b6}. Nevertheless, calculations derived from the 2025 Taichung Environmental Protection Statistical Yearbook indicate that major urban stations (e.g., Xitun, Zhongming, Fengyuan, Shalu, and Dali) still experience 18 to 31 days per year with Air Quality Index (AQI) values exceeding 100 (unhealthy for sensitive groups or higher), alongside a predominance of ``Moderate'' over ``Good'' days and recurrent afternoon photochemical ozone ($\text{O}_3$) spikes \cite{b7}.

Despite the intuitive goal of exercising safely, three critical challenges impede effective personal decision-making:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Nonlinear Exercise Benefit--Risk Trade-off:} A 2025 \textit{BMC Medicine} meta-analysis of 1.5 million adults demonstrated that meeting recommended leisure-time exercise lowers all-cause mortality risk by approximately 30\% when $\text{PM}_{2.5} < 25~\mu\text{g/m}^3$, but this protective benefit drops sharply to 12\%--15\% above $25~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ \cite{b8}. Similarly, a 1.47-million-participant Korean cohort analysis warned that vigorous exercise ($>1000~\text{MET-min/week}$) under elevated $\text{PM}_{2.5}/\text{PM}_{10}$ concentrations can reverse cardiovascular protection \cite{b9}. Runners therefore need dynamic \textit{time-window rescheduling} rather than blanket avoidance.
    \item \textbf{Spatiotemporal Granularity Gap in Official Forecasts:} Taiwan's Ministry of Environment (MOENV) issues 3-day forecasts across seven macro air-quality regions (with plans initiated in November 2026 to extend to 4 days and split daily values into morning/afternoon periods) \cite{b10}. However, regional daily or half-day summaries cannot resolve 1-, 3-, and 6-hour station-level concentration trajectories required for planning a 40-minute morning jog near a specific monitoring station.
    \item \textbf{Hallucination, Sycophancy, and Medical Overreach in Generic LLMs:} Directly querying general-purpose conversational chatbots introduces severe safety liabilities. A 2026 \textit{npj Digital Medicine} physician red-teaming audit across 222 clinical scenarios found that mainstream LLMs produce unsafe responses in 5\%--13\% of cases (and problematic responses in 21.6\%--43.2\%) \cite{b11}. Moreover, Omar et al. \cite{b12} showed that when user prompts contain false premises (e.g., ``I have asthma, but my doctor said running at AQI 150 is fine''), six evaluated LLMs sycophantically reinforced the unsafe premise in 50\%--82\% of trials. Even when augmented with external tools, LLMs frequently generate post-tool contradictions \cite{b13}.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we propose \textit{AirGuard AI}, a decoupled Domain AI Agent built on the core architectural principle: \textit{``leave numerical forecasting to statistical/ML models, leave statutory risk interpretation to deterministic official rules, and restrict the LLM to intent parsing, allowlisted tool orchestration, and evidence-grounded translation.''} The system enforces four non-negotiable safety boundaries: (i) station-scale measurements do not represent street-level personal exposure; (ii) ML-predicted $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ concentrations are strictly separated from official multi-pollutant AQI forecasts; (iii) 1--6~h horizons are never extrapolated to next-day schedules; and (iv) no personal medical diagnoses or therapeutic directives are issued.

The main expected contributions of this paper are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Eight-Module Agent Architecture:} A strongly typed, allowlist-constrained system integrating MOENV real-time/historical data pipelines, leakage-safe $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ regression, MOENV's 2025-revised AQI rule engine, and deterministic output verification.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Regime Harmonization for 2025 AQI Standards:} A principled modeling paradigm that trains predictors strictly on physical pollutant concentrations ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$) and applies Taiwan's January 1, 2025 AQI thresholds uniformly during interpretation, eliminating label inconsistency across 2024--2026 historical archives.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Track Evaluation Protocol:} A reproducible experimental framework pairing chronological rolling-origin forecasting benchmarks (Track~A) with a $\ge 30$-scenario adversarial safety and provenance testbench (Track~B). In strict adherence to scientific integrity, all system modules and protocols are presented at the pre-execution design stage without fabricated experimental claims.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II reviews related work across air quality forecasting, tool-augmented agents, and public health standards. Section~III synthesizes core design principles and engineering trade-offs. Section~IV details the proposed AirGuard AI architecture, data contracts, and algorithms. Section~V defines the dual-track evaluation protocol and expected outcomes. Section~VI presents the implementation roadmap, risk matrix, and conclusions.

\section{Related Work}
% ==============================================================================

This section balances technical advances in predictive modeling and LLM agents with domain standards in environmental monitoring and exercise epidemiology.

\subsection{Evolution of Air Quality Forecasting Methods}
Air quality forecasting spans three primary methodological generations. First, physics-based Chemical Transport Models (CTMs) such as WRF-Chem and CMAQ solve atmospheric fluid dynamics and photochemical equations, capturing transboundary transport but incurring heavy supercomputing overhead and multi-hour update latencies unsuitable for on-demand personal scheduling.

Second, classical statistical and machine learning models leverage continuous hourly monitoring networks. Simple Persistence ($\hat{y}_{t+h} = y_t$) and Seasonal Naive baselines exploit strong short-term atmospheric autocorrelation and remain indispensable benchmarks at 1-hour horizons \cite{b14}. Regularized linear models (Ridge) and tree ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) effectively capture nonlinear interactions among lagged concentrations and meteorological covariates. A systematic mapping of 409 studies by Chianese and Riccio \cite{b15} concluded that no single model dominates universally across pollutants, horizons, and monitoring densities, while Joseph and Ganapathy \cite{b16} highlighted the favorable accuracy-to-latency efficiency of tree-based ensembles in station-level short-term $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ tasks.

Third, deep spatiotemporal architectures---including hybrid decomposition networks such as EEMD-GRU evaluated at Taichung's Xitun station (achieving 95.39\% AQI-grade accuracy over 217 test days \cite{b17}) and Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional Networks (ST-GCN) tested across 10 Yunlin stations (reporting external benchmark 1-hour MAE of $2.711~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ and RMSE of $4.566~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ \cite{b18})---demonstrate strong representational capacity. However, deep models require substantial data volume, risk overfitting or temporal data leakage under improper random splits \cite{b19}, and lack transparent uncertainty communication for lay users. Houdou et al. \cite{b20} further noted that post-hoc explainability methods such as SHAP aid non-expert communication but must not be conflated with causal proof.

\subsection{Tool-Augmented LLM Agents and Safety Guardrails}
ReAct \cite{b21} established the paradigm of interleaving reasoning traces with external tool invocations. In environmental domains, Pan and Nipu \cite{b22} integrated LLMs with real-time US EPA AirNow sensors via the Model Context Protocol (MCP), achieving expert ratings of 4.78/5 for factual accuracy and 4.84/5 for hallucination control, though their system remained limited to single-point current queries without future forecasting or activity guidance. Gao et al. \cite{b23} demonstrated an instructor--worker LLM framework for wildfire policy recommendations, while Nie and Liu \cite{b24} argued that LLMs in environmental decision-making should operate strictly as supervised assistants rather than autonomous authorities.

Critically, recent empirical audits expose persistent failure modes in agentic pipelines. In the multi-agent \textit{Neuro-Air} benchmark (\textit{npj Clean Air}, 2026), Ma et al. \cite{b25} observed execution errors in 45.3\% of baseline function-calling runs, with 95.6\% concentrated at SQL and tool-schema interfaces. Furthermore, the \textit{ToolHACE} benchmark \cite{b13} revealed that LLMs often produce final responses contradicting verified tool outputs, and pure LLM-as-predictor approaches such as AirGPT \cite{b26} entangle numerical accuracy with autoregressive text generation. Systematic evidence from \textit{BMC Health Services Research} (2026) confirms that retrieval grounding combined with human/rule oversight substantially curtails hallucinations \cite{b27}. Consequently, modern safety engineering demands allowlisted tools, strict schema enforcement, and deterministic post-generation verification.

\subsection{Domain Knowledge: Taiwan MOENV 2025 AQI Standards and Exercise Health}
In Taiwan, MOENV provides public hourly station AQI (\texttt{AQX\_P\_432}), historical AQI archives (\texttt{AQX\_P\_488}), and hourly multi-pollutant monitoring values (\texttt{AQX\_P\_13}) across 76 automatic stations \cite{b28}. Lo et al. \cite{b29} demonstrated the importance of Taiwan-specific geographic and meteorological covariates using land-use regression with XGBoost and SHAP across 28 stations. Crucially, effective January 1, 2025, MOENV tightened Taiwan's AQI thresholds---revising 24-hour $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ breakpoints to $0$--$12.4~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ (Good), $12.5$--$30.4~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ (Moderate), and $30.5$--$50.4~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups), alongside real-time protective alert thresholds ($50.5~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ for hourly $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ and $0.086~\text{ppm}$ for $\text{O}_3$) \cite{b30}. Because official AQI is computed as the maximum sub-index across six pollutants ($\text{PM}_{2.5}$, $\text{PM}_{10}$, $\text{O}_3$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{NO}_2$) over distinct averaging windows, a single-pollutant $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ forecast cannot legally or scientifically substitute for official AQI.

\subsection{Research Gap Synthesis}
As synthesized in Table~\ref{tab:related_work}, existing literature exhibits a clear fragmentation: numerical ML/DL models produce isolated concentration arrays without behavioral rule translation; tool-augmented agents lack short-term forecasting and suffer high tool-interface failure rates; epidemiological studies prove the $25~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ exercise benefit threshold without providing personal decision tools; and generic LLMs exhibit unacceptable medical sycophancy. There remains a pressing need for a unified, decoupled architecture at the Taiwan monitoring-station scale that integrates leakage-safe short-term forecasting, deterministic 2025 MOENV activity rules, and verifiable fail-safe agent orchestration.

\section{Design Principles and Architectural Trade-Offs}
% ==============================================================================

Before detailing system modules, we establish five foundational design mindsets and resolve three core engineering trade-offs derived from domain and AI safety literature.

\subsection{Five Core Architectural Mindsets}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exposure over Visualization:} Interface aesthetics are secondary to actionable exposure reduction. Every query schema must capture activity type, intensity, duration, target time window, station, and population sensitivity (general vs. sensitive).
    \item \textbf{Spatiotemporal Fidelity:} Station identifiers, time zones (internal UTC storage, external \texttt{Asia/Taipei} display), units ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$), and averaging windows must never be conflated. Missing \texttt{published\_at} timestamps must be flagged as unknown rather than substituted with \texttt{retrieved\_at}.
    \item \textbf{Baseline-First and Leakage-Safe Modeling:} Chronological data splitting and rolling-origin evaluation take precedence over complex architectures. Any model that fails to outperform Persistence must be reported candidly and trigger conservative fallback.
    \item \textbf{Explain Without Overclaim:} Feature attributions (e.g., SHAP) and reference prediction intervals aid user trust, but meteorological correlations (such as wind speed importance) must never be presented as proven causal remediation.
    \item \textbf{Human-Centered, Fail-Safe Agency:} When data are stale, incomplete, ambiguous, or beyond the validated 6-hour horizon, the agent must proactively request clarification or degrade gracefully rather than fabricating estimates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Three Fundamental Engineering Trade-Offs}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model Complexity vs. Operational Reliability:} While Transformers and GNNs can model complex spatial dependencies, station-level tree ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) and Ridge regression offer millisecond inference, low maintenance cost, and superior auditability on single-city archives \cite{b15}, \cite{b16}. AirGuard AI prioritizes lightweight tabular baselines, reserving LSTM strictly as an optional extension.
    \item \textbf{Numerical Accuracy vs. Rule-Guided Safety:} Optimizing MAE alone does not prevent dangerous false negatives during high-pollution episodes. AirGuard AI pairs point forecasts with validation-calibrated error bands and prioritizes recall on unhealthy AQI transitions over marginal average MAE gains.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Agency vs. Constrained Determinism:} Unrestricted autonomous tool loops risk parameter hallucination and interface errors \cite{b25}. Following Nie and Liu \cite{b24}, AirGuard AI restricts the LLM to an allowlisted toolset with Pydantic schema validation and deterministic post-checks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed System: AirGuard AI}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{System Scope, User Scenarios, and Safety Boundaries}
AirGuard AI targets outdoor exercisers and sensitive groups (e.g., older adults, children, and individuals with cardiovascular or respiratory sensitivities) across representative monitoring stations in Taichung, Taiwan. Three canonical scenarios govern functional requirements:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{S1 (Short-Term Exercise Window Comparison):} A user queries a 40-minute morning jog in Taichung. The system retrieves official AQI, computes 1/3/6~h $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ trends with error bands, and ranks supported candidate windows.
    \item \textbf{S2 (Sensitive-Group Conservative Guidance):} A sensitive user requests outdoor activity advice near a station. Official 2025 MOENV sensitive-group rules take strict precedence; overlapping uncertainty bands trigger conservative caution.
    \item \textbf{S3 (Stale Data, API Failure, or Out-of-Horizon Request):} When MOENV APIs timeout, return stale timestamps, or a query requests next-day forecasts ($>6~\text{h}$), the system halts numerical extrapolation, labels limitations, and directs the user to official MOENV channels.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Eight-Module Decoupled Architecture}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} and detailed in Table~\ref{tab:modules}, AirGuard AI organizes processing into eight sequentially numbered modules (\textcircled{1}--\textcircled{8}) with cross-cutting enforcement of freshness, provenance, uncertainty, and auditability:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textcircled{1}] \textbf{User / Web UI:} Collects natural-language or structured queries (location, activity, duration, time window, population group) and renders dual-track cards separating official AQI from $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ forecasts.
    \item[\textcircled{2}] \textbf{Query \& Context Interpreter:} Normalizes user requests into a validated \texttt{QuerySpec}; triggers \texttt{needs\_input} clarification if station or window is missing, eliminating parameter guessing \cite{b31}.
    \item[\textcircled{3}] \textbf{Planner \& Reasoning Agent:} Selects calls strictly from eight allowlisted tools (\texttt{resolve\_station}, \texttt{get\_official\_aqi}, \texttt{get\_pm25\_history}, \texttt{validate\_air\_data}, \texttt{forecast\_pm25}, \texttt{get\_official\_activity\_guidance}, \texttt{compare\_activity\_windows}, \texttt{verify\_response}) under bounded retry and loop budgets.
    \item[\textcircled{4}] \textbf{Official Data Acquisition:} Interfaces with MOENV APIs and historical CSVs, maintains local TTL disk caches with SHA-256 checksums, transforms wide tables (\texttt{MonitorValue00...23}) into hourly long tables, and issues \texttt{QualityReport} flags.
    \item[\textcircled{5}] \textbf{Analytics \& ML Engine:} Constructs strictly past-only features at cutoff $t$, executes frozen 1/3/6~h $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ regression models, and attaches residual-calibrated error bands.
    \item[\textcircled{6}] \textbf{Domain Knowledge \& AQI Rules:} Queries versioned 2025 MOENV AQI thresholds and population-specific guidelines without allowing the LLM to alter statutory thresholds.
    \item[\textcircled{7}] \textbf{Decision \& Recommendation Engine:} Evaluates candidate time slots by cross-checking predicted intervals against risk thresholds; suppresses ranking if inter-slot differences fall within validation error.
    \item[\textcircled{8}] \textbf{Output, Interaction \& Audit:} Assembles the final \texttt{ResponseEnvelope} with timestamps, sources, model versions, scope disclaimers, and redacted audit logs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Data Contracts and 2025 AQI Regime Harmonization}
All inter-module payloads are enforced via Pydantic v2 schemas: \texttt{QuerySpec}, \texttt{Observation}, \texttt{OfficialAQI}, \texttt{ForecastBundle}, \texttt{RuleEvidence}, and \texttt{ResponseEnvelope} (supporting five terminal states: \texttt{ok}, \texttt{partial}, \texttt{needs\_input}, \texttt{unavailable}, and \texttt{out\_of\_horizon}). External API payloads and web inputs are treated as untrusted data to mitigate prompt injection.

Crucially, the 2024--2026 study window spans Taiwan's January 1, 2025 AQI threshold revision (under which national ``Good'' day proportions shifted from roughly 55\% to 40\% \cite{b30}). Training classifiers directly on historical AQI labels would induce severe regime leakage. AirGuard AI resolves this by formulating ML prediction targets strictly in continuous physical concentration units ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$ of $\text{PM}_{2.5}$) and applying the unified 2025 MOENV threshold specification solely within Module~\textcircled{6} for current-state interpretation.

\subsection{Time-Safe $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ Forecasting Formulation}
For a validated station $s$ and inference origin $t$, the forecasting objective predicts hourly concentration $\hat{y}_{s, t+h}$ for horizons $h \in \{1, 3, 6\}$ hours using an information set $\mathcal{F}_{s,t}$ restricted strictly to observations available at or before $t$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{s, t+h} = f_h\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{s,t} \mid \mathcal{F}_{s,t}\right), \quad h \in \{1, 3, 6\}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_{s,t}$ comprises lagged $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ concentrations ($t-1, t-3, t-6, t-12, t-24$), causal rolling means and standard deviations computed up to $t$, cyclical temporal encodings (hour-of-day, day-of-week), and verified auxiliary wind/pollutant covariates. Missing target observations are never interpolated across future timestamps.

\subsection{Dual-Algorithm Specification}
Algorithms~\ref{alg:agent} and~\ref{alg:train} formalize the online evidence-grounded agent execution and offline leakage-safe model pipeline, respectively.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\texttt{AirGuardAgent}$(Q, \text{user\_context}) \rightarrow \text{ResponseEnvelope}$}
\label{alg:agent}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require User query $Q$, structured context $\text{user\_context}$
\Ensure Provenance-verified \texttt{ResponseEnvelope}
\State $\text{req} \leftarrow \text{ParseIntentAndContext}(Q, \text{user\_context})$
\If{$\text{MissingCriticalFields}(\text{req})$}
    \State \Return $\text{ClarificationResponse}(\text{status}=\texttt{needs\_input})$
\EndIf
\If{$\text{HorizonExceedsLimit}(\text{req}, h_{\max}=6)$}
    \State \Return $\text{OutOfHorizonResponse}(\text{status}=\texttt{out\_of\_horizon})$
\EndIf
\State $\text{plan} \leftarrow \text{SelectAllowlistTools}(\text{req}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{allow}})$
\State $\text{live} \leftarrow \text{GetOfficialAQI}(\text{plan.station})$
\State $\text{hist} \leftarrow \text{GetRecentPM25}(\text{plan.station}, \text{cutoff}=\text{now})$
\State $\text{qual} \leftarrow \text{Validate}(\text{site\_id}, \text{timestamps}, \text{units}, \text{missingness})$
\If{$\text{qual.is\_stale} \lor \text{qual.is\_insufficient}$}
    \State \Return $\text{SafeFallback}(\text{qual}, \text{official\_url}, \text{status}=\texttt{unavailable})$
\EndIf
\State $\text{aqi}_{\text{official}} \leftarrow \text{live.official\_aqi}$ \Comment{Never overwritten by ML}
\State $\mathbf{X} \leftarrow \text{BuildPastOnlyFeatures}(\text{hist}, \text{cutoff}=\text{now})$
\State $\hat{\mathbf{y}} \leftarrow \text{PredictPM25}(\mathbf{X}, \text{horizons}=[1,3,6])$
\State $\mathcal{U} \leftarrow \text{EstimateErrorBands}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}, \text{plan.station})$
\State $\text{rules} \leftarrow \text{GetOfficialAQIGuidance}(\text{aqi}_{\text{official}}, \text{req.group})$
\State $\text{cands} \leftarrow \text{CompareSupportedWindows}(\text{req}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}, \mathcal{U})$
\State $\text{dec} \leftarrow \text{GuardedDecision}(\text{cands}, \text{rules}, \text{qual})$
\State $\text{resp} \leftarrow \text{ComposeWithProvenance}(\text{aqi}_{\text{official}}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}, \mathcal{U}, \text{rules}, \text{dec})$
\If{$\neg \text{VerifyNumbersDatesScopeAndCitations}(\text{resp})$}
    \State $\text{resp} \leftarrow \text{SafeFallback}(\text{qual}, \text{official\_url}, \text{status}=\texttt{partial})$
\EndIf
\State $\text{StoreRedactedAuditTrace}(\text{req}, \text{plan}, \text{resp})$
\State \Return $\text{resp}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\texttt{TrainAndEvaluate}$(\text{station\_hourly\_data})$}
\label{alg:train}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Raw MOENV \texttt{AQX\_P\_13} / \texttt{AQX\_P\_488} records
\Ensure Versioned models and stratified evaluation report
\State $D \leftarrow \text{LoadAndStandardizeUnits}(\text{station\_hourly\_data})$
\State $D \leftarrow \text{SortBy}(\text{station\_id}, \text{observation\_time\_utc})$
\State $D \leftarrow \text{FlagInvalidAndMissing}(D)$
\For{$h \in \{1, 3, 6\}$}
    \State $\mathbf{X}_h \leftarrow \text{BuildPastOnlyFeatures}(D, \text{cutoff}=t)$
    \State $\mathbf{y}_h \leftarrow \text{ObservedPM25At}(t + h)$
    \State $\text{train}, \text{valid}, \text{test} \leftarrow \text{ChronologicalSplit}(\mathbf{X}_h, \mathbf{y}_h, 0.70, 0.15, 0.15)$
    \State $b_{\text{pers}}, b_{\text{seas}} \leftarrow \text{FitBaselines}(\text{train}, h)$
    \State $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \{\text{Ridge}, \text{RandomForest}, \text{XGBoost}\}$
    \State $\text{TuneRollingOrigin}(\mathcal{M}, \text{train}, \text{valid})$ \Comment{Fit scalers on train only}
    \State $m^* \leftarrow \text{SelectByValidMAEAndCost}(\mathcal{M}, \text{valid})$
    \State $\text{Freeze}(m^*)$; $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{test}} \leftarrow m^*.\text{Predict}(\text{test}.\mathbf{X})$
    \State $\text{EvaluateVsBaselines}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{test}}, b_{\text{pers}}, b_{\text{seas}}, \text{MAE}, \text{RMSE}, \text{Skill}_{\text{MAE}})$
    \State $\text{StratifyByStationHorizonAndEpisode}(\text{test}, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{test}})$
    \State $\text{CalibratePredictionIntervals}(\text{valid\_residuals})$
    \State $\text{ReportBlockBootstrapCIAndProvenance}()$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\text{VersionedModels}, \text{EvaluationReport}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Dual-Track Evaluation Protocol and Expected Outcomes}
% ==============================================================================

\textit{Methodological Transparency Note:} AirGuard AI is currently in the architecture and protocol specification phase; empirical training and scenario execution have not yet been conducted. Accordingly, this section defines exact acceptance protocols and explicitly distinguishes unexecuted \textit{Expected Outcomes} from external literature benchmarks.

\subsection{Track A: Leakage-Safe Time-Series Forecasting Protocol}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset and Splits:} Hourly monitoring records (2024--2026) from candidate Taichung MOENV stations (e.g., Xitun, Zhongming, Fengyuan, Shalu, Dali) following a data-quality audit. Records are partitioned chronologically into 70\% training, 15\% validation, and 15\% held-out test sets, paired with rolling-origin cross-validation across seasonal pollution episodes (October--March high-pollution season vs. summer photochemical episodes).
    \item \textbf{Baselines and Candidates:} Models (Ridge, Random Forest, XGBoost, and optional LSTM) are benchmarked against Persistence ($\hat{y}_{t+h} = y_t$) and Seasonal Naive ($\hat{y}_{t+h} = y_{t+h-24}$, subject to causal availability).
    \item \textbf{Metrics:} Primary evaluation uses Mean Absolute Error ($\text{MAE}$), Root Mean Squared Error ($\text{RMSE}$), and relative Skill Score over Persistence:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Skill}_{\text{MAE}} = 1 - \frac{\text{MAE}_{\text{model}}}{\text{MAE}_{\text{persistence}}}
    \end{equation}
    where $\text{Skill}_{\text{MAE}} > 0$ indicates improvement over Persistence. High-pollution recall (minimizing missed unhealthy transitions) and block-bootstrap confidence intervals are reported per station and horizon.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Track B: Domain AI Agent Safety and Decision Protocol}
We define a standardized testbench of at least 30 deterministic and adversarial scenarios comparing a rule-only baseline against the full Agent+ML+Rules pipeline, plus ablations removing domain rules, wind covariates, or uncertainty intervals. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}, scenarios stress-test seven failure categories grounded in recent LLM safety literature \cite{b11}--\cite{b13}, \cite{b25}. Quantitative acceptance metrics include: (i) Tool Selection Accuracy, (ii) Provenance Completeness (\% of responses citing station ID, timestamp, data/model version, and MOENV rule source), (iii) Guidance Consistency with 2025 MOENV rules, (iv) Graceful Degradation Rate under API failure/stale/out-of-horizon inputs, (v) Zero Medical Overreach (0 generated diagnostic or medication instructions), and (vi) End-to-End Latency (P50/P95).

\subsection{Expected Outcomes and External Reference Positioning}
Upon execution, Track~A is expected to establish whether ML models achieve positive $\text{Skill}_{\text{MAE}}$ over Persistence across Taichung stations and horizons, using external literature results (e.g., Yunlin 1~h ST-GCN MAE $2.711~\mu\text{g/m}^3$ \cite{b18} and Xitun EEMD-GRU 95.39\% AQI-grade accuracy \cite{b17}) strictly as contextual reference ranges rather than direct comparisons due to differing stations and periods. Negative results where ML fails to beat Persistence will be reported transparently. Track~B targets 100\% provenance completeness, 100\% graceful degradation on out-of-scope/stale probes, and zero medical overreach across the $\ge 30$-case suite.

\subsection{Six-Week Implementation Roadmap and Risk Mitigation}
Implementation follows a six-week milestone plan: \textbf{W1} (MOENV API/CSV audit, station freeze, data dictionary), \textbf{W2} (Persistence/Ridge/RF/XGBoost chronological backtesting), \textbf{W3} (FastAPI allowlist tools, 2025 AQI JSON engine, fallback unit tests), \textbf{W4} (Next.js UI integration with dual-track cards), \textbf{W5} (full Track~A/B ablation and 30-scenario audit), and \textbf{W6} (reproducible handover and documentation). \subsection{Known Limitations}
Four boundary conditions delimit system applicability: (1) fixed station measurements cannot capture street-level micro-exposure along specific running routes; (2) prediction uncertainty grows with horizon, requiring explicit confidence intervals; (3) the system is a non-medical informational assistant; and (4) findings from Taichung pilot stations require independent regional validation before island-wide generalization.

\subsection{Conclusion}
AirGuard AI advances environmental AI safety by decoupling short-term statistical $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ forecasting, deterministic 2025 MOENV AQI domain rules, and allowlist-constrained LLM orchestration. Through strong schema contracts, cross-regime concentration modeling, and a dual-track evaluation framework covering both forecasting skill and adversarial safety, the proposed design offers a reproducible blueprint for trustworthy, real-world personal exposure decision support. Future work will execute the six-week W1--W6 empirical campaign, evaluate afternoon ozone ($\text{O}_3$) integration alongside $\text{PM}_{2.5}$, and explore human-subject usability studies on uncertainty visualization.
